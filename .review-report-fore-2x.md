## Decisions

- **Block grid.** Art pixel X lands at `plan.screen.x + k·(X − ART_ORIGIN)`. The blocks are rigid with the item, and the art always rasterises around the same integer anchor, so no block edge ever moves relative to the item and nothing crawls.
  - I did **not** also snap the anchor to multiples of k. That would make the item move in k-px jumps.
  - The test checks the stated property: edges keep the same phase relative to the item across 1 px camera moves.
- **Drop tail.** The brief said to draw it as a chunky line after the blit. Instead the window includes `DROP_TAIL` above the body, clipped to the view's top, so the art's own chain or cord is upscaled with the rest.
  - The scratch stays bounded: at most view/k high.
- **Fixed art anchor.** The anchor sits at ART_ORIGIN whatever is visible. Hash-textured art (leaves, sheets) therefore cannot change pattern as the window clips.
- **Wire coverage.** The wire span is left out of the coverage estimate (like clouds). Counted, its view-wide box alone was over 45 %.
- **Rims.** The existing rim widths (about 1–2 art px, so 2–6 screen px) are kept. Only their colour changed.

## Likeliest not to compile / unverified

- Closures in `ForePal::for_theme`: `build` captures `shades`, which captures `tint`. This should be fine.
- `std::mem::take(scratch)` needs `Scratch: Default`. It is derived.
- Doc links to private items (`[`TARGET`]`, `[`Scratch`]`) may warn under rustdoc. They will not fail the build.
- Every test is unverified. The tests most likely to need numbers adjusted:
  - `a_span_blitted_end_lands_inside_its_wall`: the scan finds the outermost lit pixel on rows ±6 around the anchor, over a black floor.
  - `a_grating_keeps_its_holes_after_the_upscale`.
  - `the_scratch_does_not_grow_after_the_first_frame`: the Flatbed clock is held, because movers change their windows.
  - The old `a_pellet_under_an_item_is_never_overwritten`: the pellet now sits in the 10 px rat core, so it should still pass.
- Cost and bytes are unmeasured. Each k×k block costs k² `blend_screen` calls, so a view-filling item is about the same number of screen writes as before. The art rasterises about 1/k² as many pixels.
